# Milestone 4: Airport and models

The goal from `fovea-plan.md`: flattened runways, a glTF model library, and hand-placed KHSV landmarks. Models stand on the milestone 3 terrain, loaded on the paging thread and drawn at 60 Hz; milestone 7's entities will use the same model library.

As before, each step is its own pull request. Steps 1 and 2 run in WSL and CI; steps 3 to 5 also need the Windows machine for the desk, and step 4 its Blender.

## The data (checked 2026-11-07)

- **Runways:** the FAA's surveyed runway ends (latitude, longitude, elevation, NAVD88) are already in `tools/check-elevation.py`, from the FAA's airport data as AirNav publishes it; the widths (150 ft each, as published) are to be taken from the same data in step 1. US Government work, public domain; the FAA joins `ATTRIBUTION.md` and the database's credits.
- **Landmark positions:** read off the NAIP imagery already in the database (public domain), not taken from OpenStreetMap: OSM's licence (ODbL) asks share-alike of derived databases, and its servers couldn't be reached to check its coverage of the airport's buildings. OSM can follow for taxiways, aprons and footprints if wanted, with its notice in `ATTRIBUTION.md`.
- **Models:** none suitable is public and free of restrictions, so Fovea makes its own, generic ones (D-025). Blender 5.2 is on the Windows machine.

## Decisions (accepted 2026-11-07, in `decisions.md`)

- **D-022 Runways are smoothed in the compiler, from the elevation itself.** The compiler takes each runway's rectangle from a committed airport file (FAA ends and widths) and, inside it, replaces the finest level's heights with a smooth surface fitted to the 1 m lidar: smooth along the runway, keeping its grade changes, and across it, keeping its crown (a 1 to 1.5% cross slope puts the edges 0.2 to 0.35 m below the centreline), blended into the surrounding ground over the shoulders. Coarser levels are filtered from it as now, so their recorded errors stay true. This removes the lidar's noise and anything on the runway when it was flown, without moving the surface off the survey; a straight line between the surveyed ends, or a level cross-section, would not. Taxiways and aprons follow later.
- **D-023 Models are glTF 2.0 binary (`.glb`), read by `libs/models` with cgltf.** A CPU-only library like `libs/terrain` (no Vulkan), so its parsing is tested without a GPU and a host could use it too. cgltf (MIT) is one file with no dependencies, and writes glTF too (for tests). fastgltf is faster, but needs simdjson, and parsing is a small part of loading a model, done once per model on the paging thread.
- **D-024 Model textures are decoded and uploaded uncompressed, for now.** The paging thread decodes a model's PNG or JPEG textures (stb_image, now built with JPEG too: D-012 left texture formats to this choice) and builds their mips; the GPU holds RGBA8. The model files stay plain glTF, straight from Blender, and a few dozen landmarks take little of the memory budget. Compressed textures (BC7 offline, as for terrain, or `KHR_texture_basisu`) are revisited in milestone 6, when vegetation and many models make memory and uploads matter.
- **D-025 Models are Fovea's own and generic, built by committed scripts.** Each model is a Blender Python script in `data/models/` that builds it and exports a `.glb`; the scripts and the small `.glb` files are committed (own work, under the repository's licence). Landmarks are massing models: the right footprint, height and colour, not detail. Each glTF material names its Fovea material in `extras` (the plan: material IDs from day one), checked when it loads.
- **D-026 Placements are a tile layer.** A committed placement file per database lists each landmark (model, latitude, longitude, true heading). The compiler checks each model is in `data/models/` and bakes them into a `placements` layer (D-018) of the tile containing each, at a level the settings choose and the manifest records, with the lowest terrain height under the model's footprint (models carry a plinth below their ground line, so the uphill side isn't buried and the downhill side doesn't float). Placements are drawn while their tile is resident, not only while it is selected: a tile stops being drawn once it splits, which near the airport is always. For KHSV the level is 12, the pinned roots, so placements are always there; finer levels page them with their tiles when an area has more.

## Step 1: Runways in the compiler

- `data/airports/khsv.toml` (committed): each runway's ends (FAA latitude, longitude, elevation) and width, with their source; `tools/check-elevation.py` reads its runway ends from it rather than its own copy. The compiler smooths the finest level's heights inside each runway and blends the shoulders (D-022); coarser levels follow by the existing filter. The FAA is credited in the manifest and `ATTRIBUTION.md`.
- **Done when:** `tools/check-elevation.py` fits its own smooth surface to the lidar on each runway (independently, in numpy) and the database is within 10 cm of it on the runway, nowhere bumpier than a stated change of slope; the surveyed end elevations still match within 0.30 m; the shoulders meet the ground with no step; the comparison with the sources leaves out the runways and shoulders, and every coarse tile is still within its recorded error.

- **Result:** `data/airports/khsv.toml` holds both runways as the FAA publishes them (AirNav, checked 2026-11-07: ends in degrees-minutes, NAD83, elevations NAVD88; 150 ft wide; 12,600 and 10,001 ft long, checked against the ends to 0.5%), and `[runways]` in `data/compile/khsv.toml` names it, blending over 20 m. The compiler (`apps/fovea-compiler/runways.cpp`, no GDAL) samples the sources every metre over each runway, before the finest level, and fits a surface: a station every 2 m, each a local regression over 30 m either side (tricube weights) of a quadratic along and a crown across (v, |v| and v^2, so a peaked or rounded crown and a cross slope all fit), robust as LOWESS is (four passes of bisquare weights from each sample's residual against the whole surface, scaled by the whole runway's spread). Inside the rectangle the finest level's heights are the surface; beyond it, the sources' plus the surface's correction at the nearest edge point, fading out over the blend (smoothstep). Coarser levels are filtered from that as before. KHSV's 1 m lidar is already smooth on the runways: 5 to 7 mm RMS from the fit, at most 7 cm, so the database's runways moved by millimetres; what changed is their bumps.
- **Checked:** `tools/check-elevation.py` now reads the runways from the airport file and fits its own surface (a station every 5 m, equal weights over 25 m either side, the same crown terms, refitted without samples past 4 robust standard deviations), and leaves the runways and their blend out of the comparison with the sources. On KHSV: the database within 2.1 cm of the script's fit (p99 4 mm); the slope changes between 5 m chords along the centreline and edges at most 0.24% (the sources: 0.5 to 0.96%), the largest in the first 20 m of 18R, where the runway's own end crests (the sources show it; elsewhere at most 0.18%; the limit is 0.3%); the runway's profile kept (grade changes between 30 m chords up to 0.5% on 18R/36L, as in the sources); across the blend the database less the sources at most 4.8 cm, changing at most 1.1 cm per 0.5 m, and past it within 1 cm of the sources; the surveyed ends and the field elevation within 2 cm, as before; every other check as before. Unit tests (`CompilerRunways.*`, on ground made up as a function of metres along and across): the fit within 2 cm of the ground's grade and peaked or rounded crown under ripples and a 2.5 m bump (exact without them); the blend starts at the surface, keeps a 4% shoulder's own slope and ends at the ground; neighbouring tiles agree and others are untouched; through the compiler on rough ground only the runway's samples change, edges are shared and the roots' errors still bound their children; and mistakes in the airport file (a mistyped end, a missing width) are refused.
- **Found on the way:** robust weights computed station by station locked onto the 2.5 m bump wherever it filled much of a station's window (2.1 m of it left in the surface); weighed against the whole runway's residuals, as LOWESS does, it drops out at once. And the first blend held the edge's height flat outward and faded it into the ground: where KHSV's shoulders fall away at about 4%, that left a lip 0.2 m above the ground 8 m out (found by the checker's blend test); blending the correction instead keeps the shoulders' own slope.
- **Review:** fixed: runways under 5 m wide were accepted though the crown's terms can't be fitted to fewer than six samples across (now refused); crossing runways would each be fitted alone, the second leaving a step on the first (now refused, with a message, until needed; KHSV's are parallel); a syntax error in the airport file was also reported as "no [[runway]]"; the runway fit and the tiles each added the geoid their own way (now one function); and in the checker, the margin round the runways assumed 257 samples a side, its fit's last stretch held the last station's grade flat, and a runway sample with no source would have crashed it. Not changed: an object across the whole runway for longer than the fit's window was said to make it singular and stop the build; a test of 3 m over the full width for 80 m shows it doesn't (the fit takes it as the ground there, and is unaffected 60 m away), so the fallback first added for it, which nothing reached, was taken out again. The survey checks now need the airport file, as that is where the surveyed ends are. The checker takes 15 s on KHSV, not the minutes feared.

## Step 2: The model library

- `libs/models` (a new library: `models ← core`, and `render ← models`, in `architecture.md`): reads a `.glb` (cgltf): meshes (positions, normals, texture coordinates, indices), materials (base colour factor and texture, the Fovea material ID), the textures (PNG or JPEG) decoded with their mips. The compiler doesn't link it: it only checks a placement's model file exists. Allocates as it likes: it runs on the paging thread.
- **Done when:** tests round-trip models written with cgltf (geometry, materials, textures), reject damaged and unsupported ones (with a reason), and require a material ID.
- **Result:** `libs/models` (`fovea/models/model.hpp`; `models ← core` in `architecture.md`, the include rules updated) reads a `.glb` from memory or a file with cgltf 1.15 (MIT, pinned by SHA-256, compiled once in its own small library) into what the renderer will upload: one vertex array (position, normal, texture coordinates) and one index array, primitives drawing ranges of them, materials (name, Fovea material ID, base colour factor and texture, alpha mode and cutoff, double-sidedness), textures (an image and its wrap modes) and images with their full mip chains (2 x 2 boxes, an odd edge joining the last box, colour averaged in linear light). A model is flattened through its nodes' transforms into its own frame (glTF's: metres, +Y up, +Z its front), normals by the inverse transpose and a mirroring transform's triangles turned back; articulated parts wait for milestone 7. The Fovea material ID is a material's extras `{"fovea_material": 0 to 255}` (a Blender custom property), required on every material. Refused, each with a reason: files that aren't `.glb`, external files, required extensions (Draco, for one), primitives other than triangles, or without normals or a material, a textured material's primitive without texture coordinates, a texture that isn't a PNG or JPEG of at most 8192 pixels a side, indices past the vertices or not in threes, and models over 256 MB, 16 M vertices or 48 M indices. `libs/core` gains `decode_image` (PNG or JPEG in memory, its size checked from the header before decoding), `encode_png` and `encode_jpeg`; stb_image is now built with JPEG too (D-012, D-024).
- **Checked:** unit tests on `.glb` files the test writes byte by byte: a textured triangle read exactly (vertices, indices, material, wraps, mips); JPEG textures; translation and scale applied, and a mirroring, shearing matrix whose normals stay unit and whose front faces still agree with them; unindexed triangles indexed in order; the material ID required and malformed ones refused; each refusal above; 500 files with random bytes changed read or refused, never worse (under ASan and UBSan too); mips of a black and white checker 188 in sRGB (50% in linear light), not 128; and in-memory PNG and JPEG with the size limit. Planted bugs (no winding fix under mirroring, mips averaged in sRGB) each fail their test.
- **Review:** fixed: an image used by several textures was decoded for each, with no limit on the decoded total, so a small file of one 8192-pixel JPEG and a thousand textures naming it would have asked for hundreds of GB (images are now decoded once, kept apart from the textures that sample them, `Model::images`, and a model's decoded images are limited to 1 GB); sparse indices read as all zeros through cgltf, giving invisible triangles and no error (now refused); a buffer's declared length was allocated before it was checked, a terabyte for a few bytes of data URI (now refused when larger than the file); the material ID was taken from the first `"fovea_material"` anywhere in the extras, nested groups of custom properties included (now the top-level key only); skinned meshes were placed by their node's transform, which glTF says they ignore (refused until milestone 7); mips raised every source texel's channels to a power (now a table, `srgb_to_linear` and `linear_to_srgb` moved into `libs/core/colour.hpp` from the compiler, which uses them too); reading whole files was written a third time (`read_file` in core, for models and PNGs; the terrain reader keeps its own for now); and `read_png` had come to read JPEGs and refuse images over 16,384 pixels a side (PNG only again, any size). Each has a test, but the 1 GB limit (four 8192-pixel images would take 1.4 GB to show it).

## Step 3: Drawing models

- The renderer draws models: their meshes in shared vertex and index buffers, textures in a bindless array, each placement an instance placed relative to the eye from double precision (as tiles are), lit like the terrain. The paging thread loads a model the first time a placement needs it; the render thread draws only loaded ones and never waits.
- **Done when:** golden images of written models on written terrain match in CI (lavapipe), a model stands on the terrain where its placement puts it (within a pixel of where the view's matrix, in double, puts it), and drawing allocates nothing.
- **Result:** the database gains placements (`libs/terrain`): a `placements` tile layer (each a model name, latitude, longitude, ellipsoidal height of its ground line and true heading; names are letters, digits, `_` and `-`, so a name can't leave the database), `[layers.placements] level` in the manifest, and the models as `models/<name>.glb` in the database. `load_terrain` reads the placements from the root tiles and reports them; placements at a finer level are reported as not drawn yet (D-026 has them paged with their tiles, for an area with more than its roots hold: #48). The paging thread loads each placed model once (`libs/render/src/vk/models.cpp`), between tiles, through `libs/models`, and stages its vertices, indices, materials and images in the same staging ring and batches as tiles, the render thread taking up to 4 a frame with its tiles' uploads. Models share one vertex buffer (64 MB), one index buffer (64 MB), one material buffer and one bindless array of images (1024), with a sampler for each pair of wrap modes. Each view draws every visible placement's primitives (a sphere around the model against the frustum) with one indirect draw: a command and a record each, the record the placement's frame (glTF's +Y along the ellipsoid normal, +Z its heading, +X its left) and its origin relative to the eye, made in double on the CPU (`shaders/model.slang`). Lit by the terrain's sun with light from the sky (0.35 + 0.65 of the sun); both sides of each triangle drawn, a back face's normal turned round; mask materials discard below their cutoff, blend materials are drawn opaque for now. The device now also asks for `multiDrawIndirect`, `drawIndirectFirstInstance` and `shaderDemoteToHelperInvocation` (`discard`; all Vulkan 1.3 drivers have it, lavapipe and NVIDIA the other two). Stats gain models loaded and placements drawn; `fovea` prints the placements at load and models that couldn't load at exit.
- **Checked:** GPU tests on written databases, under validation, on lavapipe and the RTX 5070 Ti: a 40 x 10 m slab set 6 m to its model's left and 15 m forward, placed at heading 90, seen from above, shows within a pixel of where the view's matrix, in double, puts its top's corners (a heading turned the wrong way, a mirrored frame, or the eye offset left out each fail it); a model that isn't there and one that doesn't read are both reported, by name, and the third drawn; 50 placements of two models draw with no allocation on the render thread; and a golden image of towers and textured boxes on hills (`tests/data/golden/models_on_hills.png`, lavapipe; NVIDIA within the 1% the terrain golden allows). The placements layer round-trips, and every truncation, extra byte, bad name, point, height or heading is refused.
- **Review:** fixed: models that couldn't be set up (more placements than the renderer takes, or no memory for their buffers) stopped the terrain loading at all (now reported, and the terrain drawn without them); tiles at a placements level finer than the roots had their placements read and thrown away (read only at the roots' level now), and a bad placements layer replaced a tile's other problem rather than adding to it; the paging thread loaded every model before any tile (now one model between every few tiles); the shared vertex and index buffers took 64 MB each whatever the models (now twice the models' files, from 1 to 64 MB); placements are capped at 4096 for now, as each is tested against each view every frame, and those visible but past the draw records are counted (`placements_dropped`); a failure flag nothing read, a wrapper around the model, and the model draw written twice are gone; and the placements encoder now refuses what the reader would (a bad name would have written a layer the runtime refused). Found with these, under validation: once models arrive between tiles, a model can arrive after frames have drawn others, and its copies into the shared buffers must wait for those frames' shader reads (a barrier now; the golden test showed the hazard). Each has a test (but the interleaving, which the golden test exercises).

## Step 4: KHSV landmarks

- Blender scripts for the KHSV landmarks (the control tower, the terminal, hangars and a few large buildings), placed from the imagery in `data/placements/khsv.toml`; the compiler bakes them into the database (D-026).
- **Done when:** on the desk each landmark stands on its footprint in the imagery, at the right height, from the CIGI eyepoint, and the owner finds it looks right.

## Step 5: Hardware check

- Fly the KHSV routes on the desk with the landmarks: frame timing, model loading, and the look of the airport from low altitude. Results recorded; anything unexpected becomes an issue.
